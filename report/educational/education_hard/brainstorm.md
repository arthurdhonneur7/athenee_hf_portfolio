# Prompt

You are an expert in finance portfolio construction etc.

I am currently building a new product that will look a lot like a fund of hedge fund, and I would like you to help me draft an explanative report that I can show my partners and my investors in order to explain what we are doing.

I will provide you with the rational and many of my research findings, and after I guess we can go from there, you can help me build my report, which part goes where etc.

- In a nutshell the idea is diversification from market beta with niche strategies that produce uncorrelated returns. Indeed we invest in a bunch of hedge fund that are producing uncorrelated returns, have a close to equal weighting on them, and then see the vol get crushed.

- someone could advocate that some hedge funds will appear uncorrelated and could in the end finish by some correlation spikes. This is why I am very very careful in strategy selection, in stress testing scenarios with the portfolio managers and the black swans. I know that there will still be a small part of them that will pass the test and give some poor performance and still have some tail events, but this is why we do diversify into more than 10 funds, in order to make it manageable that a fund or two have -20% returns (with 10 funds would produce a -4% return for the fund and the other funds could perfectly be up and thus tame this bad one, and two doing -20% looking at the selection we do is really catastrophic scenario ).

- we have done many research, first on diversification. I we take an asset that has a mean return of 10% and a vol of 18%, and we simulate a wealth path over and over with monte carlo simulation and look at statistics. We can see that on a 10 year horizon we have a median MDD of 30% and a 25th percentile of 40%. This is inline with the sp500 ( 10 ret and 18 vol ), and sp500 is still a good investment. But what if we had 10 independent copies of the sp500 yielding 10 and 18vol, but in a uncorrelated fashion ? We can simulate this portfolio, equal weight with monthly rebalancement, we would have a median MDD of 5% and 7% 25th percentile, all of this on a 10y horizon. Indeed we are able to divide by 5.5-6 the MDD scenario using diversification. On a risk adjusted basis this would be, even with a 10% average return an incredible product. This is the motivation for what we do. On the vol reduction we might have a vol of 18% going to a vol of to a vol of 6% ( scaling of 1/sqrt(N) )

- now someone could advocate that hedge fund do spike to 1 correlation anad that is not realistic. A study we did on a data base of more than 800 funds, all in the JPM hedge fund database that is available for introduction in their cap intro plateform. Test period is 2020-2023, out of sample is 2023-2026. The idea is to find a criterion, like on the test period find 75% percentile on sharpe ratio, select these funds in a separate basket ( here 200 ). Then we create equal weighted portfolios, drawing N assets from these 200, and compute the metrics. We do this monte carlo simulation over and over. Like this we have thousands of random portfolio drawn from the initial criterion, and we wonder if this has ex ante predictive power, and if the resulting portfolio are indeed diversified and produce interesting results. It does, I could elaborate more but this is the idea of this part. We do see that vol gets crushed, we see good ret / vol ratio, and we believe that our selection process for the FoF will likely put us in the top 15% top 10% of these portfolios.

- now we have understood that we diversification is powerful, that hedge funds using JPM data could actually deliver these wonderful risk adjusted products, now our benchmark would be something like the top25% of these monte carlo simulation on JPM. Now where do we find hedge funds ? Cap intro plateforms from banks. Hedge funds need Prime brokers and banks are these PB, they do introductions between hedge funds and allocators like us. We have successfully been talked with JPM, SG, BNP, Barclays, Citi, UBS, GS. Now also you have third party marketers, people helping promising funds market, which acts like a good filter. Also have my own "mapping of the universe", where I talk with large funds and take a lot of meetings just to see what everyone is doing. More than 150 meetings with HF managers, and much more pitched product ( only take a meeting if the product is promising ).

- our selection process for the funds and what we found to be the best metrics to look when selecting a fund. We look closely to risk management, this is the most important thing, we need to make sure that they have a "Drawdown budget". We love funds with high turnover, as the more you trade on a potential advantage you have on the market, the more we will see if it is statistically significant. We love funds that are purely market neutral, and that are built like this, not funds that sometimes go net long as they see "opportunities", as we don't really believe on market timing, at least very hard to do. We don't like funds that trade large cap stocks, as the more capital in a product, the more efficient it is, and we don't really believe that people are able to time the market ( out of 1000 PMs, surely 20-50 will have an amazing track, but we do expect this anyway to be luck ), not shaming them but even if they did had skill would be very hard to differentiate them from luck. So we do like people trading niche markets or niche strategies that are capacity constrained. We love profiles like spin offs of big prop trading desks ( BNP deutsche GS etc ), of big pod shops like Millenium or any big 4, as you know that these people have Risk management embedded in them, and will not try you to sell you noise and lucky trading, indeed the DD budget at these pod shops are more like 5-7%, so this is brutal and they know what they do.

- now a very important step is to make sure that the funds are really uncorrelated to equity markets and to each others. So we have several metrics that we look, first the returns of the fund versus the top 10% of the sp500 returns. If we see something bad we pass. We then look at the alpha and beta versus sp500, the most important is a lot of alpha that is significant ( t > 2 ), and idealy with a 0 beta. Sometimes we might consider strategies that have a non zero beta, like 0.2 if they have an alpha of 20% + pa. Then we also look at the correlation, between -0.2 and 0.2. We also look at upside and downside capture versus sp500, ideally the funds have a inverted sign upside vs downside capture, let's say 0.5 upside - 0.5 downside. Then between them we want to make sure that the funds are not correlated so we look at the correlation matrix, and monitor closely.

- Now that we have many funds that produce some nice results, we know that the DD and vol are crushed by 5.5-6, we will do an example that is conservative but that will be clear. Let's say we have funds that have a long term sharpe ratio of 1.5 ( conservative as we tend to select better funds ). We would like them to run for example a vol of 20%, with a net return on their side of 30%. The DD on a 10y period as we said before could be expected individually to be 40%, but if the uncorrelatedness step is well done, we expect a theoreticall reduction of 5.5-6 times the DD, but to be conservative let's say that we will have a 4 time reduction, and thus a MDD of max 10%. The vol will go to approximately 6% let's say. As I said these are theoretical and still conservative results, as some funds that we have selected do have incredible sharpe ratios and are able to provide some crazy good returns.
